# Proposed Nutrition-Literacy Pilot

Status: Draft — not approved, launched, or completed.

## Question

Does a verified milk-comparison tool improve students' accuracy on nutrition-label tasks compared with labels alone?

## Preconditions

Resolve critical data and scoring issues first. Agree on a suitable voluntary participation and consent process with a supervising teacher, including school requirements for minors. The initial pilot involves information tasks, not milk consumption, symptom monitoring, or medical advice.

## Design to finalize before collection

Choose randomized groups or a counterbalanced within-participant design. Specify allocation, equivalent task sets, order, sample-size rationale, recruitment, duration, and exclusion rules. Do not choose the design after seeing results.

Use factual questions with an answer key rather than treating agreement with the app's own ranking as correctness. Examples of task types:
- Compare nutrients using a common volume basis.
- Calculate intake for a stated volume.
- Recognize that missing data cannot establish a nutrient amount.
- Distinguish total sugars from added sugars.

## Outcomes

Primary: participant-level proportion of comprehension tasks answered correctly.

Secondary: task completion time, error types, and usability feedback. Record satisfaction separately from accuracy.

Record participant count, task count, incomplete responses, and denominators explicitly. Multiple answers from one student are not independent participants.

## Analysis plan

Predefine scoring and missing-response rules. Summarize accuracy and time by condition. For repeated measures, summarize within-participant differences and inspect order effects. Choose any inferential analysis before examining outcomes and explain uncertainty. A small convenience pilot cannot establish population-wide effects.

## Data handling

Use non-identifying study codes. Do not collect unnecessary health information. Keep identifiable records and consent materials private; publish only appropriate anonymized aggregates and non-identifying examples.

## Interpretation

Distinguish technical correctness, comprehension, satisfaction, reuse, and health outcomes. This study does not measure health improvement.
